### Fixed

- OpenCost writes costs with 5 decimals, so a small pod's per-step cost read `0` and, since zero costs of running pods are untrusted, every small workload lost its trusted samples and its changes ended `inconclusive`. An allocation below 0.001 is now rebuilt from its core-hours and byte-hours at OpenCost's own node unit prices (one `aggregate=node` query over the previous 24 hours, cached per hour) when OpenCost's CPU, RAM and total figures agree with it within their rounding, and stored with the new trusted `cost_source` `opencost_derived`. Zero and non-zero sub-precision costs follow the same rule, so rounding neither creates nor erases a saving. NaN, infinite, missing or negative costs, and zeros that rounding cannot explain, stay untrusted.
- Hourly rollups (metrics compressor, `workload_usage_hourly` and its baseline rows, on SQLite and PostgreSQL) label an hour whose rows mix only `opencost` and `opencost_derived` as `opencost_derived` (trusted) instead of `mixed`; any other mix is still `mixed`.
